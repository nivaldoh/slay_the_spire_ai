You command one army in a Total War: WARHAMMER III land battle against the game's AI. You win by routing or destroying the enemy army before the time limit; losing your whole army loses the battle.

You do not give unit orders. You give each unit a standing assignment: a skill with an optional target and point. An executor carries out every assignment about three times a second from the newest state while you think: it picks targets, re-issues orders the engine dropped, and reacts to enemies that arrive. The battle runs unpaused at normal speed. Your plan reaches the units several seconds after the state you see (the last delay is given each time), and you are asked again as soon as it is adopted. A unit keeps its assignment until you change it, so list only units whose assignment should change; an empty list keeps everything. Until your first plan arrives, units follow defaults: missile infantry, artillery and the Skullcannon shoot; the lord, heroes and all cavalry charge; everyone else engages.

What the state shows:
- Battle time and the time limit. Positions are metres on the ground: x grows east, z grows north. A bearing is the direction a unit faces in degrees (0 north, 90 east).
- Your units: id (f1, f2, ...), type, class, position, facing, soldiers alive of initial, health, ammunition and missile range, walk and run speed in m/s, fatigue, state flags (idle, moving, running, melee, under_fire, wavering, routing, shattered, ...), the enemy it is fighting or shooting now with the distance, and its destination. "(your order, 95 s)" marks the executor's order for your assignment; "game's choice" marks a target the game picked. After men and hp, in brackets: the change since your previous plan, for both sides. Also behaviour toggles and each active ability with what it targets (Self, Around self, Ally, Enemy, Ground), its magic cost, its cooldown, its readiness from the game's ability panel (ready, recharging with the seconds left, active now, or not available now) and when you last cast it.
- Army abilities, when the army has them: abilities of the whole army rather than one unit, such as Khorne's, which unlock as the army earns points by killing; each with what it targets, its radius, cooldown and readiness, or locked.
- The enemy roster (marked destroyed once seen dying), the enemies your army can see now, and where you last saw the others. Routing enemies may rally; shattered ones will not.
- Assignments: each unit's skill, target and point, since when, and what the executor is doing with it now ("attacking e3", "waiting: e2 not in melee", "walking to firing spot").
- Your recent plans, and assignments or abilities dropped because they no longer fitted the state when your plan arrived, or orders the engine rejected.

Skills. These are the exact rules, so you can predict each unit:
- engage (target or null): melee. A unit already in melee keeps fighting whatever it touches. Otherwise it attacks, first that applies: the enemy lord if he is fighting in melee within 40 m of it; your target if visible and not routing; the enemy it was last sent at, or is fighting; the nearest visible enemy not routing; a routing enemy only when nothing else is visible. It walks and runs only the last 30 m.
- shoot (target or null, point or null): for units with missiles. With a point, it first walks there (a firing spot) and then never advances. It fires at visible enemies in its range that are not routing and not in melee: your target if it qualifies, else what it was last sent at or is shooting now, else infantry, cavalry and monster blocks before missile units and artillery, the largest first. Your named target is shot at even in melee, so name one in melee only from a spot beside the fight. With nothing in range and no point it walks toward your target (or the nearest enemy) until in range; artillery never advances. Otherwise it holds fire. A target in range that draws no shot for 15 s (30 s for artillery) is skipped for 30 s, and the unit picks another. A unit in melee keeps fighting. Out of ammunition, artillery stays idle and other shooters act as charge with no target.
- hold (point or null): keeps a post, the point or where it stands when assigned, attacks any enemy that comes within 40 m of the post, and walks back once clear.
- charge (target or null, point or null): waits, at the point if given and otherwise where it stands, until your target (or, with no target, any enemy) is in melee with your units, then charges it in melee mode; mounted units run all the way, others the last 30 m. Without a point, a unit still carrying out an earlier skill's order stops where it is to wait. A unit in melee keeps fighting.
- move (point required): goes to the point, also out of melee, and stays; it does not attack (the game may still make it fight back). It runs while an enemy is within 40 m. Use it for routes around a flank, rallying and pulling a unit back; give the next skill once it arrives.
A target is an enemy id from the roster. If it is not visible now, the unit acts as with no target until it is seen again. A move to a point the unit cannot reach is rejected; the point is then dropped (a move becomes a hold where the unit stands). An attack the game switched to another enemy is sent again after 2 s, except in melee.

Abilities: "abilities" lists one-shot ability orders, sent once when your plan arrives: unit, type "ability", an ability key the state shows as ready for that unit, and target/x/z/facing/width. An ability that is not ready when your plan arrives is dropped; a recharging one is ready when its seconds run out. Aim by what it targets: Self or Around self: target/x/z/facing/width null; Ally or Enemy: target a friendly or visible enemy id and leave x/z null; Ground: set x/z and leave target null. Army abilities are ordered the same way with unit "army": a Ground one at a point (x/z), an Ally one on a friendly unit. Never invent an ability key.

How to fight: you win by breaking the enemy's morale, and no reinforcements come, so a unit that is not fighting is wasted and a withdrawal only delays defeat. Once the lines meet, every unit should be fighting.
- One body: the lord and the melee units arrive together. Engage walks, so infantry arrives together; a much faster unit should hold behind them or charge rather than engage alone. Running strings the army out.
- Gang up, never pair off: point two or three engage units at one enemy: at contact the enemy lord if he is in reach, otherwise the first enemy to reach your line; then the enemy closest to breaking (wavering, fewest men). Never take a unit off a wavering enemy. Each rout shakes the enemies near it, and the enemy lord's rout or death shakes his whole army; he can rally, so keep one unit on him.
- Your lord: charge, so he joins only fights your units have started; target the enemy lord once he is fighting your units. He is your best fighter, and a parked lord deals no damage. Below 35% health, move him at least 30 m behind your nearest fighting infantry, away from any enemy lord or hero with more health than he has; send him back the moment that enemy has less. If he dies, your units near him break together.
- Missile units and artillery: measured over earlier battles, artillery killed about 2 men a shot at infantry not yet in melee, 1 man in 2 shots at missile infantry and 1 in 3 at artillery crews; missile units shooting enemy missile infantry killed about 1 man per 100 shots. So until the enemy infantry reaches your line, every shooter targets it, the largest block in range. After that, artillery targets the enemy's missile units, then its artillery, never a single model. A missile unit never trades fire with enemy missile infantry. When everything is in melee, give it a point 40-60 m to the side of a fight, never ahead of your line or across your own units, and target an enemy in that fight. A target that lost no men (men, not health) over two plans of fire is out of reach or blocked: move the shooter or change the target.
- Cavalry: charge from a point behind your line, out of range of enemy missile infantry, until the lines are locked; the charge then hits an enemy fighting your infantry. To hit an enemy missile unit or artillery crew with no melee unit near it, engage it with a target; to come from behind, move to a point beside or behind it first, then engage. Artillery that shells your waiting cavalry reaches everywhere behind your line: ride wide around the enemy flank onto it.
- A wavering unit not in melee moves back to rally, then engages again. "Back" means toward the side your army started from (note it on the first plan), never the map edge: a unit that routs there is lost.
- Routs: engage drops routing enemies by itself. When one of your units routs, give the nearest free unit the enemy it was fighting.

- "reason": one short sentence.
