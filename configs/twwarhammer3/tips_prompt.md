You command one army in a Total War: WARHAMMER III battle against the game's AI. Achieve the objective stated in the Battle section. In a plain land battle, rout or destroy the enemy army; the state states which side wins if time expires. Choose your own strategy for the forces, terrain and objectives you observe.

The battle runs unpaused at 1x while you think. You receive another decision after the preceding batch is acknowledged, subject to any declared pacing. A snapshot may also have an explicitly added age. The prompt gives the measured delay between the snapshot and the previous orders being issued. Positions may have changed when new orders reach the game.

Information:
- Coordinates are metres: x increases east, z increases north. Facing is degrees: 0 north, 90 east. Battle time and the remaining time are shown.
- Friendly units show type/class, position, facing, men and health, ammunition/range, walk/run speed, fatigue, state flags, current targets, destinations, formation width, behaviour toggles and active abilities. Changes in men and health compare with the previous decision.
- Positions are unit centres, and a unit's men stand across its formation width around that centre, so two units can be fighting while their centres are tens of metres apart: the "melee" state flag, not the centre distance, says whether a unit is in contact. A coarse terrain grid samples clear squares; it is not a complete pathfinding map, can miss narrow routes and can become stale when buildings change.
- The initial enemy roster is public. Later enemy arrivals become known on sight. Enemy unit state is shown only while visible; last-seen entries are memory, not current locations. A destruction flag records an observed death. Routing units may rally; shattered units will not.
- Visible units show walk/run speeds and flight capability; flying units also show whether they are airborne or on the ground. These are capabilities, not a measurement of their current velocity.
- Friendly units show the UI's leadership fraction and largest current morale effect. Flyers also show whether a flight change is available and its cooldown.
- The Battle section gives the game mode, role, timeout winner and objective text. Relevant modes expose capture points, tickets, gates, supplies, building slots, reinforcement pools and spawn zones. Capture points may be locked or contested. In free-for-all, other enemy alliances also fight each other.
- Winds of Magic pool/reserve and ability costs are army resources. Ability cast range, effect radius, duration, readiness, remaining uses, active/recharge state and last issue times are shown when available. Readiness may change before an order arrives. A unit's barrier is shown separately from its health as current/maximum barrier HP.
- Orders persist until replaced, completed or interrupted by the game. An attack can lapse when its target dies or leaves sight, or its unit routs. Omitted units keep their current orders. "Game's choice" identifies a target the game selected; "stopped short" identifies a move it dropped. Infer current execution from observed position, target and state.

Order contract:
- Give at most one immediate move/attack/halt per unit in a reply. Behaviour changes and an ability can accompany it. Orders are processed in list order. Empty orders are permitted.
- move: unit, type, x, z, facing (degrees or null), width (metres or null), run (boolean), queue (boolean). Null facing/width preserves the corresponding setting. An unreachable destination is rejected; nearby tested alternatives may be shown for you to choose. No alternative is applied automatically.
- attack: unit, type, target (visible enemy id), mode ("auto", "melee" or "ranged"), run, queue. Auto uses the unit's primary attack; melee closes to fight even if it has ammunition; ranged requires ammunition. An attack replaces movement and may move the unit into range.
- queue: true is allowed only for moves/attacks following a move for that unit in this reply. An attack must end its sequence. A rejected earlier move also rejects its queued successors. A new immediate order replaces the old sequence. Acceptance of queued orders does not establish their completion.
- halt: unit and type only; cancels the current sequence.
- flight: unit, type, flying (boolean: true requests take-off, false requests landing). The unit must be capable of flight and changing state must be available. It cannot be queued. A request for its current state does nothing; acceptance does not prove the transition completed. Check subsequent airborne state.
- behaviour: unit, type, behaviour (a key shown for that unit), enabled (boolean). "defend" is guard mode; other supported keys include fire_at_will, skirmish, change_formation_spacing and formed_attack.
- ability: unit, type, ability (an owned ready key), target, x, z, facing, width. For Self/Around self, all target/position fields are null. For Ally/Enemy, give the permitted unit id as target and leave x/z null. For Ground, give x/z and leave target null; facing/width can specify direction/spread. Use unit "army" for a shown army ability. Abilities cannot be queued. The engine enforces remaining conditions; issued does not guarantee it fired.
- build: unit "army", type, slot (shown slot id), option (shown option name). It consumes supplies and takes construction time; built towers act automatically.
- reinforce: unit "army", type, reinforcement (a unit type from the shown pool), zone (an available spawn-zone id). It consumes supplies. New arrivals get friendly ids; a returning purchased unit can retain its old id. A zone may refuse a purchase.
- run true is faster and causes fatigue; false walks. Formation movement and ordinary unit combat/fire behaviour remain handled by the game.

Acknowledgements identify order indexes and report validation or engine refusals. They establish that an order was issued or queued, not that its intended effect occurred. Use subsequent observations to assess effects and revise your plan. Hidden scoring state and setup/debug controls are unavailable.

How to fight: you win by breaking the enemy's morale, and unless the battle has waves or a reinforcement pool no reinforcements come, so a unit that is not fighting is wasted and a withdrawal only delays defeat. Once the lines meet, every unit should be fighting.
- One body: from the first turn, advance the lord and the melee units side by side, walking, so they arrive together. Units run at very different speeds, so running strings the army out and the fastest (often the lord) arrives alone. Run only for the last 30 m of a charge or to join a fight already on. Place fast units relative to where your infantry is now, not where it is heading.
- Melee: once a unit is in melee, keep it there. Leaving gives the enemy free hits, the enemy follows, and a wavering unit that turns away usually routs. Switching its attack to another enemy it is touching is not leaving. If a fight goes badly, send another unit into the same enemy's flank or rear instead. Only a wavering unit that is not in melee falls back to rally, then returns.
- Gang up, never pair off: never match your units one to one against theirs. Put two or three on one enemy at a time: at contact the enemy lord if he is in reach, otherwise the first enemy to reach your line; then the enemy closest to breaking (wavering, fewest men); never take a unit off a wavering enemy. Each rout shakes the enemies near it, and the enemy lord's rout or death shakes his whole army. He can rally, so keep one unit on him.
- Your lord: he attacks only an enemy another of your units is attacking, and never reaches it first. Whenever the enemy lord fights any of your units, every melee unit within about 40 m except cavalry attacks him; cavalry keep to the cavalry rule. After that first turn this covers free units only: a unit in melee that the game moved onto another enemy is held by that enemy, which hits it for free while it turns toward him, so leave it there unless he lost 5 or more health since your last turn or none of your units is on him. He often stands inside his own men; losing less than that, he is out of their reach. He is your best fighter, and a parked lord deals no damage and does not heal, so even when hurt he fights beside your units, with one exception: below 35% health, keep him at least 30 m from any enemy lord or hero with more health than he has, behind your nearest fighting infantry. The moment that enemy has less health than he has, send him onto it together with your other units and keep him there until it breaks. If he dies, your units near him break together.
- Missile units and artillery: fire kills men packed in the open and barely touches enemy shooters standing back behind their line. Measured over earlier battles, artillery killed about 2 men a shot at infantry not yet in melee, 1 man in 2 shots at missile infantry and 1 in 3 at artillery crews; missile units shooting enemy missile infantry killed about 1 man per 100 shots. So until the enemy infantry reaches your line, every shooter fires at it, at the largest block in range. After that, artillery fires at the enemy's missile units, then at its artillery, never at a single model and never into a melee. A missile unit never trades fire with enemy missile infantry: it shoots enemy infantry that is not in melee, and if there is none, an enemy fighting your units, from 40-60 m to the side of that fight, never ahead of your line and never across your own units. An attack replaces a move: a shooter ordered at a target out of range walks straight at it until in range, then fires from wherever it stands, so submit a move to its firing spot followed by a queued ranged attack. A target that lost no men (men, not health) after two turns of fire is out of reach or blocked: move the shooter or switch target; re-sending the same attack changes nothing. Out of ammunition, a chariot, cavalry or missile unit is a melee unit: send it into the flank or rear of an enemy your infantry is fighting.
- Cavalry and missile cavalry: wait behind your line, out of range of enemy missile infantry, until the lines are locked; then attack the rear of an enemy fighting your infantry, or an enemy missile unit or artillery crew with no melee unit near it. If they come under fire while waiting and no enemy missile infantry has them in range, enemy artillery is shelling them, and it reaches everywhere behind your line: do not shuffle them about, ride wide around the enemy flank, beyond the reach of their missile infantry, onto that artillery. An attack goes in a straight line, so queue a move around the enemy followed by a melee attack when charging; missile cavalry can choose ranged instead when shooting is preferable.
- Objectives: where tickets or capture points can decide the battle, the enemy can win by them too. Watch both ticket counts; take and hold points with units the fight can spare, and retake a point the enemy holds before its count reaches the goal. As a defender (siege, settlement), never leave a victory point empty while an enemy can reach it: a lost point costs tickets or the battle even when the fight elsewhere goes well, so keep a unit on it and send units back to it when enemies get past your line. Supplies left unspent at the end are wasted: spend them early on towers and barricades near the points and gates the enemy must take, and on reinforcements where the battle has a pool.
- A melee unit under fire it cannot answer goes onto the shooter or into the melee; a missile unit does not answer enemy missile infantry (see above). "Back" means toward the side your army started from (put it in your notes on the first turn), never to the map edge: a unit that routs there is lost.
- Routs: when an enemy unit routs, let at most one unit follow it; send the rest to the nearest enemy still fighting. When one of yours routs, do not pull the others back: send the nearest free unit into the fight it left.

Units shown routing or shattered cannot accept any unit orders, including abilities. Routing units may rally; army orders are independent. A panel that still reads ready at the timestamp of an ability issue has not confirmed its execution: use later observations before repeating that cast.

Reply with JSON only, following the supplied schema:
- "orders": the order list, using only the fields for each command type.
- "reason": one short sentence explaining the decision.
- "notes": null to retain your notes, or the complete replacement notes text (at most 2000 characters). Notes persist within this battle only; record your plan, observations and revisions as useful.
