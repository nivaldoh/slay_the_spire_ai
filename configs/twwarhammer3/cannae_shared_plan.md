Shared prebattle plan, version 1. Win the native battle and preserve the army where
possible. Every legal engine win counts, including timer wins or an alternative tactic.
This is a supplied plan for an execution study, not a required sequence or a score rule.

The centre starts projected toward the enemy. Left and right infantry begin behind its
ends; the decisive cavalry wing has two regiments and the containing wing has one.
Everyone knows the fixed ownership map, lobby rosters, starting terrain and rendezvous.
The first able regiment in each group's declared order is its anchor; routing, shattered,
dead or absent regiments cannot anchor. If none is able, no local contacts or channel
transmission/reception are available, but the group retains its own telemetry.

Centre: keep an effective infantry fight while the wings create an opportunity. Holding,
shortening, reinforcing or withdrawing are choices, not an atomic feigned-retreat action.
Tell nearby peers if the centre can hold or needs help. Do not sacrifice it just to follow
the opening plan. Lords are absent in this version; one ordinary regiment commands.

Left/right infantry: support the centre, turn into the enemy's flank when useful, and
retain room to respond to an outer threat. Read local movement and reports. Do not treat
the other wing's silence as a live report or assume that both should commit simultaneously.

Decisive cavalry: use the local cavalry advantage, then choose pursuit, assistance to
the containing wing or an attack on the infantry rear. A routed enemy may rally. Leaving
a fight early can be useful; winning it is not automatically the army's best next action.

Containing cavalry: keep enemy horse from freely reaching the infantry or report inability
to contain it. You are not assumed to win alone. Seek help or disengage when that preserves
a useful force. No commander may directly order a teammate to carry out its request.

READY means prepared for the next local task; HOLD requests preserving the present job;
COMMIT reports or requests commitment to the shared opportunity; HELP requests support;
ABORT warns that the enclosure plan may no longer be useful. These symbols convey no
unwritten coordinates or ground truth. Use a courier for a richer report, its age and
the requested consequence. Each recipient still judges what to do from its own information.

Couriers initially use each group's published starting-anchor point as rendezvous. A
moving commander may miss one. Returning there has a tactical cost; decide whether it
is useful. Signals and visible local movement may suffice instead. Exact clocks and this
preparation may support a fixed schedule; successful independent play remains a win.
