# CARLA scenario evaluation reference

[Reference index](../benchmark-references.md). Read this profile for triggered situations,
event records, termination rules and scoring incentives.

CARLA Leaderboard evaluates autonomous driving along predefined routes with traffic,
weather and scripted hazards. It is close to WH3 in having multiple actors, uncertain
events and a goal that can be reached through different local decisions. This profile
uses Leaderboard 2.1; the older 2.0 score is a historical comparison, not the same metric.

## How tasks are created

The [official overview](https://leaderboard.carla.org/) describes routes through different
road settings and scenario types based on an NHTSA traffic-situation typology. A scenario
type is a situation such as a merge, crossing pedestrian or emergency vehicle; a route
combines several concrete situations into a larger driving task.

Public routes are shipped as [XML configurations](https://github.com/carla-simulator/leaderboard/blob/cfecdc8d99f933a09b27176829ce7b8d63d66d8d/data/routes_devtest.xml).
The [parser](https://github.com/carla-simulator/leaderboard/blob/cfecdc8d99f933a09b27176829ce7b8d63d66d8d/leaderboard/utils/route_parser.py)
reads route points, weather, scenario types, trigger positions, other actors and parameters.
The behaviour of each scenario type is explicitly programmed: for example,
[DynamicObjectCrossing](https://github.com/carla-simulator/scenario_runner/blob/d7bcaf0dee05fdfd720bda45740b8c9f11ee478a/srunner/scenarios/object_crash_vehicle.py)
sets up actors and starts a crossing from trigger conditions using a behaviour tree.

This is a library of authored situation templates instantiated through configuration,
with variable parameters and simulated actors. The inspected source does not establish
whether every coordinate in every supplied route was selected manually or generated by
an authoring tool. Runtime construction or random traffic is not evidence that the
underlying scenario families were automatically invented.

## What evaluation measures

The [evaluation criteria](https://leaderboard.carla.org/evaluation_v2_1/) report route progress,
infractions and a combined driving score. At the pinned 2.1 revision,
[StatisticsManager](https://github.com/carla-simulator/leaderboard/blob/cfecdc8d99f933a09b27176829ce7b8d63d66d8d/leaderboard/utils/statistics_manager.py)
computes each route's score as its completion percentage multiplied by an infraction
factor. Fixed infraction values accumulate additively and contribute a factor
`1 / (1 + accumulated_value)`; off-route-lane travel has a separate adjustment. The score
is not a simple fraction of routes successfully completed.

Global score sums are divided by the planned route count (`_total_routes`), with standard
deviations and infraction counts per distance driven also reported. An incomplete run
must not be read as a mean over only successful routes. Multiplying mean route completion
by mean penalty does not reproduce the mean driving score. Raw infraction lists and route
statuses remain available alongside the aggregate.

Route criteria can end an attempt for deviation, being blocked or a timeout. The
[evaluator](https://github.com/carla-simulator/leaderboard/blob/cfecdc8d99f933a09b27176829ce7b8d63d66d8d/leaderboard/leaderboard_evaluator.py)
also records sensor rejection, agent initialization/runtime errors and simulator crashes.
These named cases are useful references for designing explicit WH3 statuses; their
particular penalty and replacement rules are not WH3 decisions.

## Why the score version matters

The [maintainers' 2.1 announcement](https://leaderboard.carla.org/#carla-ad-leaderboard-21)
says agents could benefit under 2.0 by ending a route early instead of risking further
exponential penalties. They changed how infractions accumulate while keeping the routes
and scenarios, and warn against comparing 2.0 and 2.1 scores directly. The final 2.1
driving score is still a nonlinear function; the implementation above is the precise rule.

For WH3, this is a concrete reason to inspect candidate health, morale and duration
formulas against deliberate waiting, retreat, sacrifice and stopping. A plausible-looking
aggregate can change the objective even when every underlying measurement is correct.

## Timing and generalisation limits

The evaluator configures synchronous simulation with a fixed simulated step. The
[scenario manager](https://github.com/carla-simulator/leaderboard/blob/cfecdc8d99f933a09b27176829ce7b8d63d66d8d/leaderboard/scenarios/scenario_manager.py)
ticks the world, queries the agent and applies its returned control before the next tick.
Agent and simulator watchdogs impose wall-time limits, but traffic does not naturally keep
advancing during inference. This loop cannot substitute for WH3's live-1x latency evaluation.

The repository supplies training, validation and development-test route files. Report the
actual routes and scenario versions used, and reserve evaluation conditions before tuning;
a public filename containing `test` does not by itself establish unseen generalisation.
Driving scores describe the specified simulated routes, not a general real-road safety claim.

## What WH3 can borrow

Within the [index's transfer proposals](../benchmark-references.md#design-lessons-to-discuss):
combine focused situations into larger tasks, trigger events from the current world state,
and separate event detection from scoring. Save a concrete scenario and a list of observed
events so a score change can be audited without inventing missing evidence.

Autonomous driving provides a direct real-world example of planning around other actors
while progress and errors both matter. WH3 shares that decision structure, but adds team
allocation and adversarial combat; it does not simulate road rules or validate driving skill.
