# WH3 battle types

Built 2026-10-14 (overnight, owner away): one minimal scenario per custom battle type
beyond the land battle, as vertical slices toward the benchmark
([benchmark.md](benchmark.md)). Each slice:
- sets the battle up unattended;
- reports the type's objectives in the state;
- gives the controllers the orders the type needs;
- ends on an engine-reported result.

Engine facts: [integration.md](integration.md#battle-types-probed-2026-10-14). Version
history: [log.md](log.md#battle-types-llm-v15-commander-v3-2026-10-14-overnight).

## How a scenario sets up

`play.scenario_steps` drives the custom battle screen:
- Every scenario selects its `battle_type` (classic by default), since the lobby keeps
  the last one; `map` selects the map.
- The lobby also keeps its slots between battles: a survival battle removes the AI slot
  and a free-for-all adds three. AI slots are therefore added or removed until there is
  one per side. These steps carry a `when` lobby expression, such as
  `CustomBattleLobbyContext.PlayerSlotList.Size < 2`, which the frontend script
  evaluates; a step whose expression is false is skipped.
- `attacker` ("player" or "enemy") swaps the teams only if the player's team has the
  other role. The player's slot starts in the defending team.
- `enemy` is one side, a list (free-for-all) or null (survival).
- A side with `reinforcements: null` also gets the lobby's autogenerated reinforcing
  army. Domination and survival refuse to start without one ("You must select some
  reinforcing units").
- If the start click loads no battle, the job stops at once with the lobby's
  `ReadyStatusText`.
- The first observation's engine type and role are checked against the scenario
  (`play.ENGINE_TYPES`, `expected_mode`); a mismatch stops the job before the battle is
  scored.

## What the state shows: `mode`

Every observation has a `mode` section. `llm` and `commander` render it as the Battle
section at the top of the state, and `digest` adds tickets and point holders to each
decision line.

| Field | Source | Types |
|---|---|---|
| `type`, `role`, `timeout_winner` | `BattleTypeState`, `SetupContext.IsPlayerAttacker`, `TimeoutWinningAllianceIndex` | all |
| `objectives` | the battle-type cheat sheet's first column: objectives and mechanics, without markup or the interface entries | all |
| `capture_points` (id `c1`..., key, name, x/z, holder, contested, locked, percent, victory) | `CapturePointList` | conquest, domination, sieges, settlements, survival |
| `tickets` per side, `tickets_goal` | `AllianceList.TicketsRemaining`, `CaptureLocationManagerContext.TicketsGoal` | conquest, domination (count up to the goal); sieges (the defender's count, no goal) |
| `gates` (name, x/z, hp %, destroyed) | `BuildingsList.Filter(IsGate)` | sieges |
| `supplies` | `PlayerArmyContext.GlobalCurrency` | sieges, settlements, domination, survival |
| `slots` (id `s1`..., type, x/z, built, building_s, usable, options with costs) | `ToggleableSlotList(PlayerArmyContext)` | sieges, settlements (survival opens them later) |
| `reinforcements` (type, cost), `spawn_zones` (id, x/z, usable) | `BattleReinforcementPoolContext.UnitsForArmy(army, 1)`, `SpawnZoneList` | domination, survival |

A spawn zone is `usable` if some affordable pool unit can be bought there now, refusing
(false) if none can, and not judged when nothing is affordable. In a free-for-all, enemy
units carry their `alliance`. Units that enter mid-battle (survival waves, purchases on
both sides) are registered as they arrive and get the next ids; a unit bought back after
it was destroyed keeps its old id.

## Orders the types add

Both are army orders (`"unit": "army"`), validated in `protocol.py`:
- `build` (slot, option): a tower or barricade on an empty slot the army can build on
  now, paid with supplies.
- `reinforce` (reinforcement, zone): a unit type from the pool, entering at one of the
  army's spawn zones. The cheapest unit of that type the engine will sell there is
  bought, then the next cheapest. The pool can hold one type at several costs, and
  positions and costs move as units are bought and returned.

A batch's builds and reinforcements share the army's supplies. Validation refuses a
zone marked refusing. In the engine, the purchase must pass `CanPurchaseUnit` and
counts only if the supplies fell.

`probe_abilities` exercises both orders. It builds the cheapest option on each usable
slot, and every 10 s it calls in the cheapest unit it can afford.

## Measures

`result.json` records `objectives` per episode:
- `victory_held`: the share of the observations the job read in which each victory
  point was ours, averaged over the points;
- `key_held` (sieges, minor settlements): the same share for the key building alone,
  the centre the owner saw left empty (from `01e3abb`);
- `supplies_left` and the `tickets` at the last observation;
- the `build` and `reinforce` orders the engine accepted.

The digest shows them. The register ([results.csv](results.csv)) has `battle_type`,
`victory_held` and `supplies_left`; older rows stay blank until `stats --rebuild`.

### The player attacking a siege

`siege-attack-kho5-dwf5` (added in `42b0c2f`) is the siege with `attacker: "player"`. The setup
swapped the teams and the checks passed: role attacker, a timeout won by the enemy, and
the enemy defender holding 1000 tickets that the attacker must take to 0. Played by
`probe_abilities` (`smoke-siege-attack-kho5-dwf5-probe_abilities-20261014-0830`, `fcba267`), it
was stopped by hand at 730 s:
- the gate was untouched at 100%;
- the defenders stood on the walls and held every point and all 1000 tickets;
- our units, down to 63 of 197 men, stood idle outside, where no attack order reaches
  units on a wall.

Attacking a fortified settlement needs what the interface lacks: gate attacks
(`attack_building`), siege equipment from the lobby, and wall positions
(`occupy_zone`). This is the first step for the attacker variants.

## Owner notes (2026-10-14)

Watching the siege smoke, played by the scripted `attack_nearest`, the owner saw the
controller ignore the goal:
- every unit fought at the gate while enemy infantry walked towards the undefended key
  building;
- the battle ended with more than 9,000 supplies unspent.

The engine still scored it won (the enemy army broke). Both are measures for the
battle-type benchmarks: who holds the victory points over time, and supplies left at the
end. Both prompts now say so, and the controllers can build and reinforce.

## Next

Toward the battle-type benchmarks, in order:
1. **More measures.** Units bought per type, and `key_held` in the register.
2. **Variants.** More maps and armies per type, starting with the `random-<type>` draws.
   Then each type at both roles where it has them. A player attacking a siege could not
   breach ([above](#the-player-attacking-a-siege)) and needs:
   - siege equipment in the lobby (`AddSiegeEquipment`);
   - wall and gate orders (`attack_building`, `occupy_zone`).
3. **Calibration per type**, as in [benchmark.md](benchmark.md#22-scenarios): which
   scripted controller wins 30-70%, and whether `skills` beats `attack_nearest`.
   Tonight's single battles already show the objective types separating controllers.
4. **Interface gaps found tonight:**
   - no deployment phase: ambushes and sieges start at once;
   - the escape zone that the ambush objective text mentions does not exist in custom
     battles;
   - the survival waves' timing is not in the state;
   - no terrain: river crossings, paths and walls are not in the state, and moves to
     unreachable points are the most common rejection;
   - some self abilities still fail in the engine ("does not support this special
     ability").
5. **A fair survival scenario.** Survival with 5 units is hopeless: lost at 198-292 s
   against waves of 20. A fair scenario needs a larger starting army or a richer pool.
