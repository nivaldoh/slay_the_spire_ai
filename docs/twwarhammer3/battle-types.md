# WH3 battle types

One minimal scenario per custom battle type beyond the land battle, as vertical slices
toward the benchmark ([benchmark.md](benchmark.md)). Each slice:
- sets the battle up unattended;
- reports the type's objectives in the state;
- gives the controllers the orders the type needs;
- ends on an engine-reported result.

Engine facts: [integration.md](integration.md#battle-types-probed-2026-10-14). History and
the per-job results:
[log.md](log.md#2026-10-14-battle-types-llm-v15-and-commander-v3-overnight).

## The scenarios

enemy, at AI very hard. The free-for-all adds the Empire army and a second Khorne army.

## How a scenario sets up

`setup.scenario_steps` drives the custom battle screen; the `battle/setup.py` docstring
describes every scenario field. What the battle types add:
- Every scenario selects its `battle_type` (classic by default), since the lobby keeps the
  last one; `map` selects the map.
- The lobby also keeps its slots between battles (a survival battle removes the AI slot,
  a free-for-all adds three), so AI slots are added or removed until there is one per
  side. These steps carry a `when` lobby expression, such as
  `CustomBattleLobbyContext.PlayerSlotList.Size < 2`, which the frontend script
  evaluates; a step whose expression is false is skipped.
- `attacker` ("player" or "enemy") swaps the teams only if the player's team has the
  other role. The player's slot starts in the defending team.
- `enemy` is one side, a list (free-for-all) or null (survival).
- A side with `reinforcements: null` gets the lobby's autogenerated reinforcing army.
  Domination and survival refuse to start without one ("You must select some
  reinforcing units").
- If the start click loads no battle, the job stops at once with the lobby's
  `ReadyStatusText`.
- The first observation's engine type and role are checked against the scenario
  (`setup.ENGINE_TYPES`, `setup.expected_mode`); a mismatch stops the job before the
  battle is scored.

## What the state shows: `mode`

Every observation has a `mode` section. `llm` and `commander` render it as the Battle
section, just after the terrain grid, and `digest` adds tickets and point holders to
each decision line.

| Field | Source | Types |
|---|---|---|
| `type`, `role`, `timeout_winner` | `BattleTypeState`, `SetupContext.IsPlayerAttacker`, `TimeoutWinningAllianceIndex` | all |
| `objectives` | the battle-type cheat sheet's first column: objectives and mechanics, without markup or the interface entries | all |
| `capture_points` (id `c1`..., key, name, x/z, holder, contested, locked, percent, victory) | `CapturePointList` | conquest, domination, sieges, settlements, survival |
| `tickets` per side, `tickets_goal` | `AllianceList.TicketsRemaining`, `CaptureLocationManagerContext.TicketsGoal` | conquest, domination (count up to the goal); sieges (the defender's count, no goal) |
| `gates` (name, x/z, hp %, destroyed) | `BuildingsList.Filter(IsGate)` | sieges |
| `supplies` | `PlayerArmyContext.GlobalCurrency` | sieges, settlements, domination, survival |
| `slots` (id `s1`..., type, x/z, built, building_s, usable, options with costs) | `ToggleableSlotList(PlayerArmyContext)` | sieges, settlements (survival opens them later) |
| `reinforcements` (type, cost), `spawn_zones` (id, x/z, usable) | `BattleReinforcementPoolContext.UnitsForArmy(army, 1)`, `SpawnZoneList` | domination, survival |

A spawn zone is `usable` if some affordable pool unit can be bought there now, refusing
(false) if none can, and not judged when nothing is affordable. In a free-for-all, enemy
units carry their `alliance`. Units that enter mid-battle (survival waves, purchases on
both sides) are registered as they arrive and get the next ids; a unit bought back after
it was destroyed keeps its old id.

## Orders the types add

Both are army orders (`"unit": "army"`), validated in `protocol.py`:
- `build` (slot, option): a tower or barricade on an empty slot the army can build on
  now, paid with supplies.
- `reinforce` (reinforcement, zone): a unit type from the pool, entering at one of the
  army's spawn zones. The cheapest unit of that type the engine will sell there is
  bought, then the next cheapest. The pool can hold one type at several costs, and
  positions and costs move as units are bought and returned.

A batch's builds and reinforcements share the army's supplies. Validation refuses a
zone marked refusing. In the engine, the purchase must pass `CanPurchaseUnit` and
counts only if the supplies fell.

`probe_abilities` exercises both orders: it builds the cheapest option on each usable
slot, and every 10 s it calls in the cheapest unit it can afford.

## Measures

`result.json` records `objectives` per episode (`play.track_objectives`):
- `victory_held`: the time each victory point was held, integrated by the mod on engine
  ticks (`method` `engine_tick_time_weighted`), divided by the observed time and
  averaged over the victory points. Older records also have controller-read `samples`,
  `held_sum` and `key_sum`;
- `key_held` (sieges, minor settlements): the same share for the key building alone,
  the centre the owner saw left empty;
- `supplies_left` and the `tickets` at the last observation;
- `build` and `reinforce`: the orders the engine accepted, and `reinforcements_by_type`.

The digest shows them, and the register ([results.csv](results.csv)) has `battle_type`,
`victory_held`, `key_held`, `supplies_left` and `reinforcements_by_type`; older rows
leave unavailable fields blank.

## Results

One battle per type and controller (`attack_nearest`, `probe_abilities` on the types
with supplies, Luna `llm` v15, Luna `commander` v3); the tables are in
[log.md](log.md#2026-10-14-battle-types-llm-v15-and-commander-v3-overnight). One battle per
cell separates no controllers, but every slice ran end to end:
- every type set up unattended and ended on an engine result, apart from one policy
  failure (fixed in `9f08da8`) and one ambush standoff cut by the job's episode timeout;
- the model controllers take and hold capture points, build and call in reinforcements;
  sieges and minor settlements were won with every victory point held;
- most rejected orders were moves to unreachable points (ambush, chokepoint,
  underground), then purchases at refusing zones (since marked) and orders to routing
  units;
- every controller lost domination (Luna `llm` on tickets, 1156 to 1500);
- survival with 5 units is hopeless: every controller lost within 198-325 s against
  waves of 20.

### The player attacking a siege

`siege-attack-kho5-dwf5` is the siege with `attacker: "player"`. The setup swapped the
teams and the checks passed: role attacker, a timeout won by the enemy, and the enemy
defender holding 1000 tickets that the attacker must take to 0. Played by
`probe_abilities` (`smoke-siege-attack-kho5-dwf5-probe_abilities-20261014-0830`), it was
stopped by hand at 730 s with the gate untouched, the defenders on the walls holding
every point and all 1000 tickets, and our units idle outside, where no attack order
reaches units on a wall. Attacking a fortified settlement needs what the interface
lacks: gate attacks (`attack_building`), siege equipment from the lobby
(`AddSiegeEquipment`) and wall positions (`occupy_zone`).

## Random draws per type

`scenarios.json` has nine random entries:
- `random-conquest`, `random-domination`, `random-ffa`;
- `random-ambush`, `random-chokepoint`, `random-underground`;
- `random-settlement`, `random-siege`, `random-survival`.

With `--scenario random-siege --seed N`, a job draws a map of that type and every side's
race. It autogenerates every army, and the reinforcing armies where the type needs them.
Classic `random` draws are unchanged: a test checks a draw recorded before battle types
existed. Four draws played once by `skills` (siege, free-for-all, survival, domination)
each set up and ended on an engine result; they are in log.md.

## Next

On 2026-10-15 the owner prioritized variety: more visibly different armies and objectives,
with 2-3 exploratory battles total per new scenario and deeper testing later. Items 1, 2
and 4 below were done in b11-b12 ([b11
log](log.md#2026-10-15-harness-b11-common-measures-and-one-report),
[below](#distinct-scenario-tranche-2026-10-16)); the rest remain candidates. Current next
steps are in [state.md](../state.md).

Every scenario must offer credible playmaking: useful strengths to exploit, disadvantages
to mitigate and choices that can materially change progress through the available
controls. Inspect these in the exploratory runs. Redesign or drop a scenario with no
credible options; preserve its evidence. Severe odds alone do not justify keeping it.

1. **First expansion.** Siege defence, survival and conquest with distinctive armies,
   maps and objectives, alongside another contrasting land army. Keep existing cavalry
   and swarm evidence; no repeat campaign is needed before adding these modes. Use the
   existing setup and adapt only necessary checks (for example, domination/survival hide
   the lobby timer that declared land benchmarks currently require).
2. **Common measures.** Casualties and survivors, whole units destroyed/alive/active,
   objective time and tickets where applicable, duration, latency, cost and failures.
   Scenario-specific scores are optional future work. These first runs check mechanics
   and produce inspectable examples, not a stable model ranking.
3. **More variety.** Domination, ambush/chokepoint, settlement, subterranean and free-for-all
   variants as useful. Random draws can provide candidates; preserve the selected army
   and settings when making a repeatable scenario. Different maps alone are not enough
   if army composition and tactical choices remain the same.
4. **Survival content.** The five-unit pilot lost in 198-325 s; try a larger starting
   force or richer pool to expose more decisions, without requiring wins or calibrating
   to a win-rate band. Wave timing is not currently in the state.
5. **Siege roles.** Defence already works and belongs in the first expansion. Attacking
   needs equipment and gate/wall orders ([above](#the-player-attacking-a-siege)); it does
   not block defence or other modes.

Other interface gaps, addressed when they obstruct a chosen scenario:
- no deployment phase: ambushes and sieges start at once;
- the escape zone mentioned in ambush objective text does not exist in custom battles;
- terrain is a coarse 32 m grid of path restrictions: walls, paths and narrow river
  crossings can fall between samples, and unreachable moves are the most common rejection;
- an ability refused with "does not support this special ability" may only lack the magic
  by issuance ([integration.md](integration.md#abilities-and-magic)); probe a refusal
  before treating it as unsupported.


## Distinct scenario tranche (2026-10-16)

b12 replaces repeated Khorne/Dwarf rosters as the source of new coverage with
Chaos Dwarf territorial defence, Kislev reinforcement budgeting and an Empire battery
evacuation under Skaven ambush (the Wood Elf recruitment cards were inactive). The Slaanesh Conquest screen is reference evidence: its
single central point cannot test the planned distributed-control question. The exact declarations
are in `configs/twwarhammer3/scenarios.json`; the
[design audit](benchmark.md#scenario-design) separates questions from map/faction variants.
Live evidence and remaining limitations are in the
[b12 log](log.md#2026-10-16-harness-b12-and-distinct-scenario-families).

Declared initial survival pools are explicit and checked by multiplicity, because the lobby sorts
them by unit class. The hidden lobby timer is ignored only when the selected mode disables
it; the battle must report no countdown. Native waves do not have a fixed lobby enemy
roster. The wall limit still ends an unfinished run as a truncation. The custom objective
uses the existing move/attack actions, with public rules and progress in `mode.custom`;
private authored placements never enter policy input. Engine outcomes and objective
success remain separate. No wave forecasting, healing/restocking or siege-attack controls
are added by this work.
